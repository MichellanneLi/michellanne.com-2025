Background

I was on the Social Impact team at Indeed, and our remit was to advance the interests of job seekers without college degrees. To that end, we sought to advance the practice of skills-first hiring, i.e., hiring based on skills rather than proxies for skills (like college degrees.)

Through this work, we discovered that 81% of employers say they prioritize skills over degrees, but 52% still recruit from degree programs. That’s because, to effectively assess candidate skills, employers need context.

Problem space

The bulleted list of skills that is standard on resumes doesn’t provide the background necessary for evaluation. Indeed’s eye-tracking studies indicate that employers first review listed skills and then seek contextual evidence within other resume sections, especially work experience, to validate those skills.

Unfortunately, job seekers without college degrees really struggle to craft resumes the effectively demonstrate their skills. That's because:

  • Many of them have diverse work histories and don’t know which skills to showcase.
  • Some pack their resumes with many skill variations for keyword matching, creating long, bloated lists which are viewed negatively by employers.
  • Some add skills that are not evidenced by their work experience.

Hypothesis

Our hypothesis was that, by helping employers evaluate skills, we could improve outcomes for job seekers without college degrees.

Helping employers evaluate skills

AI skills highlighting

I designed this concept to illustrate how we might use AI to highlight skills context on Indeed’s candidate management interface. In this design, interacting with a skill chip reveals an AI summary of how the candidate used the skill and highlights where that skill is demonstrated in their resume. Rather than relying on our old system of keyword matching, I wanted to leverage AI's ability to conduct nuanced skills analysis. This would enable us to identify relevant skills from diverse work experiences.

Findings

The results of the study demonstrated that employers did find it easier to evaluate candidates’ skills with this interface.

However, highlighting skills only worked for robust resumes that already did a good job representing those skills.

Helping job seekers represent skills

I realized that the success of any interventions on the candidate management surface would be limited because:

  • Highlighting skills and their contextual evidence is only beneficial if those skills are well represented, and we knew that job seekers who don’t have college degrees really struggle with this.
  • Most employers don’t utilize Indeed’s candidate management tools. Rather, they upload resumes from Indeed into applicant tracking systems. So, any interventions on Indeed would be limited by that practice.

Therefore, I decided to spearhead a study focused on how AI can help job seekers showcase their skills in their resumes.

I knew that AI-assisted applications were already on Indeed's roadmap, and I wanted to make sure the new suite of features included guidance on showcasing job-relevant skills as well as efforts to mitigate skills bloat.

Concepts

For this study, I developed 3 concepts that leveraged AI to analyze job seeker resumes against job postings and identify gaps in represented qualifications:

  • Option A: “Tailor your resume”: Instructs job seekers to update their resumes, organized by resume section
  • Option B: “Tailor your resume”: Instructs job seekers to update their resumes, organized by skill
  • Option C: “Employer preview”: Implicitly prompts job seekers to improve their resumes by showing them how they appear to employers on Indeed

Findings

Although I tested 3 different concepts, none of them emerged as the clear "winner." However, all of them helped me learn that:

Job seekers were open to AI assistance in adding skills and experience to their resumes. Some even requested additional AI features when adding new content to their work experience. One participant asked for an example or "some form of inspiration," while another wanted an AI assistant.

Job seekers understood the importance of tailoring their resumes, and they appreciated having a tool to do so. They believed it would make them more competitive and increase their chances of being moved forward in the hiring process. Also, they knew that the experience section is more important than listed skills.

Job seekers needed guidance around skills curation. Although most participants understood that removing irrelevant skills would help employers focus on relevant qualifications, they did not believe it was as impactful as adding more skills or work experience.

Impact

My findings proved that there was an appetite for AI tools that help job seekers better represent their skills. As a result, this key feature was baked into the systems prompt for Indeed's new AI resume builder, thus ensuring that future resumes are better poised to give employers the information they need to make skills-based evaluations.
