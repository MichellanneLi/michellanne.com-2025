Background

Aon's second largest vertical is insurance brokering, which means they work with client companies and insurance carriers to negotiate employee benefits packages. As part of this service, they offer their clients TBS (Total Benefits Solutions), an online benefits enrollment platform. TBS serves 17,000 clients with 3.5 million employees across 100 markets.

TBS was originally built in 2007. In 2020, my team was tasked with redesigning it after client feedback indicated that the existing user experience was confusing and difficult to navigate. Our clients' HR departments were overwhelmed with requests for guidance. This created an opening for competitors to threaten Aon’s market share.

Our stakeholders shared with us the following concerns:

{{> blockquote quote='New clients come back within a week asking for a user guide because employees are confused and don’t know how to use the site. They can’t navigate easily and reach out to the HR or contact center for help.'}} {{> blockquote quote='Big challenge for us is to try to help employees understand what benefits they should enroll in. We struggle with modelers or tools that tell people what they should buy. If we can crack this and truly help educate employees with meaningful information, we would be very successful.'}}

Users told us that annual enrollment was their top priority on the platform. However, the existing process was so confusing that employees frequently believe they had completed benefits enrollment when, in fact, they had not submitted their elections.

{{>blockquote quote='It is essential to be able to select benefits and options. Small bit of information with each.'}}

Goals

Based on this feedback, we articulated three primary goals for improving the annual enrollment flow:

  • Simplify the annual enrollment process by addressing friction points.
  • Educate the user about their benefits by offering key information at the right times.
  • Demonstrate the value of benefits and help users choose the options that best meet their individual needs.

Our success criteria was a reduction in poor employee feedback and customer service calls.

Challenges and Constraints

Given the scope and complexity of TBS, we identified the following challenges and constraints:

  • Reducing friction around benefits enrollment, without being able to impact the structure of benefits or enrollment requirements (those are dictated by the carriers.)
  • Building in flexibility to account for individual configurations for each of Aon's 17,000 clients.

User Flow

The old enrollment flow took a "hub and spoke" approach. The user journey began with a view of all available benefits, displayed as a "benefit matrix." After selecting and making elections for a benefit, the user was routed back to the benefit matrix. In order to complete enrollment, they needed to submit all of their elections from the benefit matrix page. This last step was frequently missed by employees, and the result was that many missed out on their benefits enrollment window.

{{> project-image style='' classes='has-shadow' img='tbs-enrolment-flow--old' alt='legacy enrollment flow' caption='legacy enrollment flow'}}

For the redesigned TBS, I proposed a linear approach similiar to one that had great success in similar products in the US market.

{{> project-image style='' classes='has-shadow' img='tbs-enrolment-flow--new' alt='new enrollment flow' caption='new enrollment flow'}}

In this linear flow, the user begins by reviewing the benefits they enrolled in the previous year. They can choose to keep, modify, or decline them. For benefits that the user wants to modify, they are guided through the process of updating their elections.

Next, the user is shown new benefits they can enroll in for the coming year. Our proof of concept design showed all of the benefits in every category on one page. This was overwhelming and created cognitive overload.

For clients that offer dozens of benefits, I created this alternate version of the new benefits page, which only displays benefits for a given category (such as health & fitness, financial wellbeing, or lifestyle.) Breaking up the new benefits over multiple pages adds steps to the enrollment flow, but reduces the cognitive load of seeing all of those benefits on a single page.

When the user has finished moving through all the benefit categories, they are asked to review and submit their elections. And, once submitted, the user is given explicit confirmation that they have completed enrollment.

User feedback

User testing on the updated enrollment flow showed that:

  • 97% of users in APAC and 98% of users in EMEA said the experience was simpler than before.
  • 98% of users in all markets felt the process was significantly improved.

Decision Support

Throughout annual enrollment, I introduced a number of decision support features to help guide the user through their elections.

Pension Contributions

On the pension enrollment page, the user is encouraged to increase their contribution to derive maximum benefit from their employer match.

The interactive retirement outlook chart enables the user to see the long term impact of their contribution choices. When the user updates their contribututions, the graph dynamically responds to their inputs.

Cost Summary

The cost summary helps the user understand how much they are spending on benefits. I created this component in response to hearing that users were adding up the costs of their benefits on pieces of paper while completing enrollment. The cost summary dynamically updates based on the user’s selections to show them their total spending and how it impacts their paycheck. The terms "Benefits Spend" and "Take Home Pay" are particular to the UK market. I worked with a UK-based subject matter expert to ensure that I was using plain language and not industry jargon.

{{> project-image style='' classes='has-shadow' img='tbs-poc-benefit-cost-summary-1' alt='cost summary with all sections closed' caption='Cost summary v1'}}
{{> project-image style='' classes='has-shadow' img='tbs-poc-benefit-cost-summary-2' alt='cost summary with first section open'}}

The cost summary evolved further when we learned that it would need to accommodate much more information than anticipated. Clients want to highlight employer costs in this space, and employee points spending needs to be displayed (in non-US markets, it's a practice for employers to allot points to spend on benefits.) I therefore moved this information out of the rail and into a slide-in panel.

Impact

During sales calls, our clients were so impressed that our biggest client added 32 new markets to their contract. User testing validated that participants completed enrollment with minimal friction, understood benefit selection, and easily navigated using the progress bar. The redesign directly addressed the support burden that threatened client retention.
